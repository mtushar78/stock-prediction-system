## 1. Strategy Philosophy & Rules of Engagement

- **Purely Educational Content:** The framework is designed strictly as a structural guide to learning technical analysis. It contains no speculative stock tips or direct buy/sell commands.
- **Capital Preservation Focus:** The curriculum focuses on systematic capital preservation over short-term tips. It warns against the high-risk "pumping and dumping" common in junk or low-paid Z-category shares where 90% of retail market participants lose capital.
- **Definition of "Long Term":** Within this philosophy, the baseline definition of a long-term horizon is a minimum of **3 years**. However, if the asset triggers an explosive momentum expansion and achieves target expectations within months, profit-taking can be executed tactically.
- **The "Techno-Funda" Filter:** Instead of scanning all 415 listed equities on the exchange, the strategy filters the universe down to a watchlist of **30 Top Fundamental Picks**. Technical indicators are only applied to companies that are sector leaders with deep competitive moats, strong brand value, and healthy structural tailwinds.

## 2. Core Technical Setup: The Four Moving Averages

Trend tracking, structure transitions, and cyclical changes are monitored on the **Daily (Day) Chart** via a system of four Simple Moving Averages (SMAs):

| SMA Period | Color | Represents |
|------------|-------------|------------|
| **200-Period SMA** | Yellow Line | Long-Term Macro Trend |
| **50-Period SMA** | Red Line | Medium-Term Trend |
| **20-Period SMA** | Purple Line | Core Trailing Stop-Loss Limits & short-term trends |
| **10-Period SMA** | Blue Line | Minor short-term momentum & initial trend shift triggers |

---

## 3. Systematic Trend Identification Criteria

The interaction between the asset price and these four structural lines dictates the overarching trend condition:

- **The Macro Downtrend:** If an asset's closing price trades below the 200-Period SMA, its primary long-term macro trend is structurally down. If it also sits below the 50-Period SMA, its medium-term trend remains down.
- **Short-Term Trend Reversal:** Confirmed when the closing price breaks above both the 10 and 20-Period SMAs, followed by a bullish crossover where the 10 SMA cuts cleanly above the 20 SMA.
- **The Perfect Momentum Bull Trend:** Characterized by a clean, stacked sequential alignment. From top to bottom, the chart must display:

  > **[Closing Price] > [10 SMA] > [20 SMA] > [50 SMA] > [200 SMA]**

  When an asset holds this stacked structure, it is confirmed to be in a perfect, accelerating uptrend across short, medium, and long-term horizons.

## 4. Execution Blueprint: Entry & Exit Rules

### A. The Three-Tranche Staged Entry Model

Rather than allocating full position sizes immediately upon a minor bounce, capital deployment is staged across structural milestones to protect downside equity:

- **Tranche 1 (Short-Term Confirmation):** Initiated when an asset breaks out of a long consolidation base, clearing local daily resistance levels with above-average volume, and triggering a 10/20 SMA bullish cross. *Example:* if an investor intends to purchase 3,000 total shares, the first **1,000 shares** are purchased here.
- **Tranche 2 (Medium-Term Confirmation):** Executed after the short-term trend holds, undergoes a minor technical pullback, and cleanly breaks out above the 50-Period SMA line. The next **1,000 shares** are deployed here.
- **Tranche 3 (Long-Term Macro Confirmation):** The final **1,000 shares** are allocated when the asset price crosses and prints above the major 200-Period SMA line. This completes the position size right as the macro bull trend is fully confirmed.

### B. The "Launchpad" Setup

The highest-probability entry setup occurs when multiple key MAs compress closely together at a horizontal support floor. When the 10, 20, 50 (and ideally 200) SMAs converge tightly into a cluster, it indicates that historical volatility has dried up. An explosive daily breakout candle bursting out of this MA cluster accompanied by high trading volume is defined as a **"Launchpad Setup,"** signifying the beginning of a powerful cyclical advance.

### C. Profit-Taking & The Trailing Stop-Loss Philosophy

- **The Traditional vs. Momentum Dilemma:** Traditional retail approaches attempt to buy at absolute lows and sell at fixed horizontal resistance targets. This approach often cuts winning trades short when an asset breaks out into a historic blue-sky run.
- **Open-Ended Target Execution:** Momentum investing does not utilize arbitrary price targets. Upside potential is kept open-ended. Instead of exiting at a resistance ceiling, the investor holds the asset and uses the 20-Period SMA as a dynamic Trailing Stop-Loss.
- **Exit Trigger:** As long as the stock price remains above the rising 20 SMA on the daily chart, the position is held. The exact moment the price crosses below and logs a closing print beneath the 20-Period SMA, a full structural exit or hard stop-loss is automatically triggered to lock in profit or limit capital erosion.

## 5. Strategic Risk Management & Capital Allocation Rules

- **Strict Diversification Floor:** Under no circumstances should a single stock position exceed **20% of total available portfolio capital**. For instance, a BDT 10 Lakh portfolio must be divided into a minimum of 5 distinct high-quality allocations (maximum BDT 2 Lakh per asset).
- **Mitigating Risk Concentration:** If an investor over-allocates capital into a single asset (e.g., placing BDT 7 Lakh out of BDT 10 Lakh into one stock), taking a necessary 5% to 10% stop-loss becomes psychologically painful. This forces the investor into the destructive cycle of repeatedly averaging down a losing trade with capital they do not have. Strict risk limits keep stop-losses small and psychologically easy to manage (limiting risk to **BDT 10,000–15,000 per trade**).
