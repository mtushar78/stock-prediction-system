## Part 1: Philosophy of a Professional Watchlist

The Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) features over 400 listed instruments. An individual retail investor cannot logically track all of them. The cornerstone of a profitable trading architecture relies on systematic filtration:

```
  [ 400+ Total Market Stocks ]
               │
               ▼  (Step 1: Monthly Chart Technical Filter)
   [ 20 - 40 Potential Stocks ]
               │
               ▼  (Step 2: Fundamental "Hygiene" Check)
   [ 10 - 15 Focused Watchlist ]
```

### Strategic Rules of Watchlist Maintenance
1. **The Core Limit:** At any given time, an investor should strictly maintain and track a maximum of **10 to 15 stocks** in their execution watchlist.
2. **Update Frequency:** The primary watchlist should only be generated or updated **once a month** (e.g., during weekend analysis). 
3. **The "One-Month Commitment" Law:** Once the monthly watchlist is locked, you must exclusively focus on those assets for the next 30 days. If the selected shares do not move for 7 to 10 days while other random stocks skyrocket, you must resist the urge to abandon your strategy. 
4. **The Danger of Churning:** Constantly switching your capital into whatever is "halted" or trending daily causes **Portfolio Churning**—a directionless cycle that degrades capital through transaction friction and poor entry timing.

## Part 2: The Technical Framework (Filtering on Monthly Charts)

Instead of using volatile lower timeframe charts for initial scanning, the framework uses **Monthly (1-Month) Charts** to confirm macro bullish structure. If a stock is fundamentally strong and establishes a macro uptrend on a monthly scale, it typically prints 6 to 8 consecutive green monthly candles, creating an enduring 6 to 8-month uptrend window. 

To make it onto the primary list, a stock must strictly fulfill the following three technical criteria on its **Monthly Chart**:

### 1. The Moving Average (MA) Rule
* **Condition:** The stock price must trade cleanly **above both the 10-period Simple Moving Average (10 SMA) and the 20-period Simple Moving Average (20 SMA)**.
* **Golden Setup:** Ideally, the 10 SMA should be crossed over and climbing above the 20 SMA. 
* **Objective:** This filters out systemic down-trends and targets stocks transitioning out of long-term accumulation phases into an active advancing markup phase.

### 2. Market Structure (Higher Lows)
* **Condition:** The price action must clearly display structural evidence of a **Lower Low (LL)** followed by the formation of at least one—ideally two—distinct **Higher Lows (HL)**.
* **Objective:** Higher lows graphically prove that downward pressure has subsided, demand is stepping up at higher price levels, and institutional accumulation ("smart money") is actively defending the asset.

### 3. Delineation of Demand Zones
* **Condition:** Connecting the established higher lows with horizontal lines or upward trendlines reveals an **Area of Interest (AOI)** or a clear **Demand Zone**.
* **Objective:** This gives the trader a defined structural floor to map out low-risk value areas on the execution timeframe.

## Part 3: The Fundamental "Hygiene" Check

Technical setups alone can lead to high-risk traps if the underlying asset lacks commercial viability. The strategy applies a strict **Capital Protection First** mindset. Speculative or low-tier companies driven solely by rumors are completely ignored. 

The technical candidates are cross-referenced against four strict fundamental rules:

| Fundamental Metric | Minimum Standard Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Market Categorization** | Must strictly belong to the **'A' Category** on the exchange. |
| **Earnings Performance** | Must demonstrate resilient or growing **Quarterly Earnings Per Share (EPS)** trends. |
| **Dividend History** | Must possess an established corporate history of paying healthy dividends in previous years. |
| **Forward Dividend Outlook** | The near-term operational outlook must project that the company will maintain its capacity to declare robust dividends for the current financial cycle. |

Combining these strict technical criteria with fundamental safeguards yields a **Techno-Funda approach**. This ensures you are exclusively trading highly liquid, fundamentally robust companies at the exact moments their institutional momentum begins.

## Part 4: Execution & Entry Mechanics (Daily Charts)

Once a stock passes the monthly technical and fundamental filters, you switch to the **Daily (1-Day) Chart** to execute trades. You do not buy immediately; you wait for a low-risk trigger point.

### The Lifecycle of an Execution Setup

#### Step 1: The Initial Rally
The stock displays a strong, visible upward movement on the daily chart. This confirms that institutional buying power is present.

#### Step 2: The Controlled Pullback
After the rally, you watch for a corrective phase. This pullback must be **controlled and orderly**—characterized by lower volatility and declining sell volume. During this correction, the asset must print structural higher lows on the daily chart, showing that smart money is retaining control.

#### Step 3: Tight Consolidation & Breakout (The Trigger)
* **Pattern Formations:** Look for a **Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)** (similar to the Mark Minervini framework), a tight flat base, or a clean consolidation channel. 
* **The Breakout Signal:** The precise entry trigger occurs when a daily candle surges out of this tight consolidation zone, clearing the **episodic pivot point**.
* **The Volume Requirement:** The breakout candle must be backed by a massive volume spike—**at least 1.5x (150%) higher than the 20-day Average Volume**. For instance, if a stock averages 100k shares daily, the breakout day must register a minimum of 150k shares.

### Risk Management Protocols
* **Stop-Loss Placement:** Place your protective stop-loss order slightly **below the most recent daily Swing Low**. Because you are entering a high-quality stock verified by the macro monthly trend, avoid overly tight stop-losses that could cause premature exits due to minor market noise.
* **Profit-Taking Strategy:** 
  * **Target Destinations:** Map out your primary upside targets using historical horizontal resistance zones visible on the chart.
  * **Partial Scaling:** As the trade moves significantly into profit (e.g., up 10%, 15%, 20%, or 25% from your entry price), systematically scale out by selling partial percentages of your position to lock in gains.

## Part 5: Trading Psychology & System Mastering

A common misconception in retail trading is that access to an exclusive stock list guarantees profits. In reality, a trading strategy only dictates a small portion of long-term success:

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 10% ── Technical & Fundamental Tools             │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 90% ── Psychology, Mindset & Execution Control   │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

To bridge the gap between analysis and profitability, every trader must be able to answer **Six Definitive Tactical Questions** before risking capital:
1. **What to buy?** (Verified via the monthly Techno-Funda framework)
2. **When to buy?** (Triggered via daily volume-backed consolidations)
3. **How much to buy?** (Determined by sizing rules and capital allocation limits)
4. **Where to cut losses?** (Calculated at the structural daily swing low before entering)
5. **How/When to take profit?** (Executed systematically using targets and percentage scales)
6. **When to hold cash?** (Knowing when market conditions are unfavorable and staying away)

### The Threat of FOMO
The greatest enemy to a trader's capital is **FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)**, driven by *Recency Bias*. Recency bias causes traders to impulsively chase stocks that surged over the last 24 to 48 hours without any structured plan. 

Success requires developing the psychological discipline to patiently wait for your specific setups to mature. If an asset on your watchlist does not hit its entry trigger, you simply do not execute the trade.
