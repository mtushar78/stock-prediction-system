## 5. Technical Framework: Monthly Charts & Stage Analysis
The underlying technical theory relies heavily on **Stan Weinstein’s Stage Analysis** mapped onto long-term monthly charts to identify macro-structural turning points:
* **Stage 1 (The Base/Accumulation Phase):** Occurs after a protracted, severe market correction. The stock stops making lower lows and forms a definitive structural floor (or "Area of Interest / Buffer Zone"). In the examples shown, the price tested the same multi-month horizontal floor 2 to 3 times without breaking down.
* **Stage 2 (The Advancing Phase):** Triggered when the price cleanly breaks out of its Stage 1 accumulation base on high volume, entering a sustained macro uptrend. 
* **The "Launchpad" Setup:** Characterized by a clear Moving Average crossover on the monthly chart involving the **10-period, 20-period, and 30-period Moving Averages**. This historical multi-month crossover indicates a powerful cyclical shift. (For example, one analyzed stock established its last valid monthly Launchpad setup in 2016, and the next structural setup did not appear until 2026—a 10-year structural cycle). A similar long-term structural setup was highlighted for *Olympic Industries*, referencing its historical 2013 launchpad cycle repeating in 2026.

* **Daily Chart Execution Tactics:** While the macro trend is filtered on the *Monthly Chart*, entry execution is strictly timed on the *Daily Chart* using three tactical setups:
  1. **Breakouts:** Buying immediately as price clears local daily resistance.
  2. **Retests:** Waiting for the price to break out and then pull back to test the broken structural ceiling as new support.
  3. **Pullbacks:** Buying minor corrective down-days within the broader daily uptrend.
* **Profit-Taking & Psychological Discipline:** Traders must use strict, pre-defined exit rules (such as scaling out at major horizontal resistance zones or using partial profit-taking mechanics). 
* **Avoiding the T+2 Churn Trap:** Short-term retail traders who constantly flip shares every 2 days over-trade, generating heavy commission expenses for brokerage houses while cutting their own profits at 8–10%.
